# Module 01 Concept Map

Coverage note: this concept order reorganizes the teaching path for beginners, but it preserves the existing Module 01 target of 87 mapped source-coverage items in JSON. This workflow file is for dependency planning only and does not change production data.

## 1. Oil in rock pores: source rock -> migration -> reservoir -> trap -> pressure.

- Prerequisites: none; this is the physical starting point.
- Terms first introduced: source rock, migration, reservoir rock, trap, seal/cap rock, pore space, reservoir pressure.
- Original Oil 101 H2 blocks:
  - Exploration and Production -> The Upstream Sector
  - Exploration and Production -> How Oil Forms: Source Rock, Reservoir Rock, and Cap Rock
  - Exploration and Production -> Reservoir Mechanics: Pressure Is Everything
- Numerical example: If a reservoir contains 1.0 billion barrels of original oil in place and later teaching uses a 30% recovery factor, only about 300 million barrels become producible supply; the physical trap matters because supply starts below oil in place.
- Market question answered: When analysts say a basin is "rich in resources," how much of that resource is actually positioned to matter for future market supply?

## 2. Reservoir to surface: wellbore, pressure drawdown, recovery factor, water/gas production.

- Prerequisites:
  - Lesson 1 pressure and pore-flow intuition.
- Terms first introduced: wellbore, drawdown, recovery factor, primary recovery, secondary recovery, decline curve, water cut, associated gas, artificial lift.
- Original Oil 101 H2 blocks:
  - Exploration and Production -> Drilling
  - Exploration and Production -> Production Phases
  - Exploration and Production -> Decline Curves and Field Life
  - Exploration and Production -> Reservoir Mechanics: Pressure Is Everything
- Numerical example: A field with 1.0 billion barrels in place yields 350 million barrels at 35% recovery but only 280 million barrels at 28% recovery, so an aggressive operating choice can remove 70 million barrels of future commercial supply.
- Market question answered: Why can a producer raise short-run output and still damage the field's long-run value and eventual supply contribution?

## 3. First surface treatment: oil/water/gas separation, dehydration, stabilization, measurement.

- Prerequisites:
  - Lesson 2 explains why the well brings up mixed fluids rather than saleable oil.
- Terms first introduced: separator, three-phase flow, dehydration, stabilization, vapor pressure, meter, lease condensate.
- Original Oil 101 H2 blocks:
  - Industry Overview -> An Industry in Three Segments
  - Components of Oil Liquids -> What Is Actually In a Barrel
  - Components of Oil Liquids -> Conventional Crude Oil
  - Components of Oil Liquids -> Condensates
  - Components of Oil Liquids -> Natural Gas Liquids In Detail
- Numerical example: If 10,000 barrels per day of wellstream arrives with 8% water and 3% flash gas equivalent by liquid volume, the stabilized liquid stream is materially smaller than headline wellstream, which changes revenue and pipeline eligibility.
- Market question answered: Why is "barrels produced at the well" not the same thing as barrels that can legally and physically enter commercial transport?

## 4. From well streams to a commercial grade: gathering, commingling, blend, custody transfer.

- Prerequisites:
  - Lesson 3 surface treatment and measurement.
- Terms first introduced: gathering system, commingling, stream, blend, grade, assay, custody transfer, terminal, marker crude.
- Original Oil 101 H2 blocks:
  - Industry Overview -> An Industry in Three Segments
  - Industry Overview -> Vertical Integration and the Disaggregation Trend
  - Crude Oil Assay -> What Is a Crude Oil Assay?
  - Crude Oil Assay -> Fields, Streams, and Blends
  - Components of Oil Liquids -> Streams, Blends, and Grades
- Numerical example: Three incoming streams of 10,000 b/d at 44 API, 20,000 b/d at 40 API, and 20,000 b/d at 36 API commingle into a 50,000 b/d blended stream at about 39.2 API, showing why the market buys a commercial grade rather than a single well's exact liquid.
- Market question answered: What exactly is being bought when someone buys WTI, Brent, Maya, or another named crude in a trading discussion?

## 5. Why crude quality changes value: density/API, sulfur, TAN, metals, salt, water, yield.

- Prerequisites:
  - Lesson 4 commercial grade and assay logic.
- Terms first introduced: API gravity, specific gravity, sulfur, TAN, metals, BS&W, salt, viscosity, yield, sweet, sour.
- Original Oil 101 H2 blocks:
  - Crude Oil Assay -> Density
  - Crude Oil Assay -> Sulfur
  - Crude Oil Assay -> Crude Quality Matrix
  - Crude Oil Assay -> Acidity, Metals, and Other Assay Items
  - Crude Oil Assay -> Price Implications
  - Chemistry of Oil -> Heteroatoms: Sulfur, Nitrogen, Oxygen, Metals
  - Chemistry of Oil -> Why Chemistry Drives Pricing
- Numerical example: Using the assay formula, 39.6 API implies specific gravity around 0.827, so a 42-gallon barrel weighs about 131 kg instead of water's roughly 159 kg; lighter oil often gives a more valuable product yield and lower transport mass per barrel.
- Market question answered: Why can two barrels that look equally "like oil" trade at very different discounts or premiums?

## 6. What a refinery actually does: separation, conversion, treatment, blending, product yield.

- Prerequisites:
  - Lesson 5 crude quality as refinery input.
- Terms first introduced: distillation, fraction, conversion, cracking, treatment, reforming, hydroprocessing, blendstock, product slate, refinery gain.
- Original Oil 101 H2 blocks:
  - Refining -> Feedstock, Slate, and Refinery Gain
  - Refining -> The Four Stages of Refining
  - Refining -> Separation: The Crude Distillation Unit
  - Refining -> Conversion: Where Margin Is Made
  - Refining -> Combining: Alkylation and Polymerization
  - Refining -> Modifying: Catalytic Reforming, Isomerization, Ethers
  - Refining -> Treatment: Hydroprocessing, Amine, Merox, Claus
  - Refining -> Blending: Where Finished Product Is Born
  - Refining -> Refinery Types and the Nelson Complexity Index
  - Finished Products -> The 19 Products of Crude Oil
  - Finished Products -> Gasoline: A Cocktail, Not a Single Product
- Numerical example: A refinery running 42 gallons of crude may sell roughly 45 gallons of products after conversion because lighter molecules occupy more volume; the volume gain does not violate mass balance, but it does change product economics.
- Market question answered: Why does the same crude barrel have different value at simple versus complex refineries and in different product markets?

## 7. How the barrel moves: pipeline, tanker, rail, truck, storage, batch and capacity constraints.

- Prerequisites:
  - Lesson 4 grade identity.
  - Lesson 6 refinery destination logic.
- Terms first introduced: pipeline, tanker, rail, truck, storage tank, batch, throughput, capacity constraint, hub, freight, last mile.
- Original Oil 101 H2 blocks:
  - Transporting Oil -> Five Methods of Moving Oil
  - Transporting Oil -> Tanker Ships
  - Transporting Oil -> Chartering and Worldscale
  - Transporting Oil -> Pipelines
  - Transporting Oil -> Crude by Rail and the Bakken Boom
  - Transporting Oil -> Trucking: The Last Mile
  - Transporting Oil -> Trading Hubs
  - Finished Products -> Global Refined Product Trade Flows
- Numerical example: If a basin produces 600,000 b/d but outbound pipe capacity is only 500,000 b/d, at least 100,000 b/d must find rail, truck, storage, or shut-in relief; the bottleneck becomes a price problem, not just a logistics footnote.
- Market question answered: Why can a region be "well supplied" in geology terms but still experience discounts, shortages, or stranded barrels?

## 8. When a barrel becomes economically usable: grade + location + timing + compatible facility.

- Prerequisites:
  - Lessons 1-7.
- Terms first introduced: compatibility, deliverability, specification window, turnaround, location differential, timing spread, facility constraint.
- Original Oil 101 H2 blocks:
  - Industry Overview -> The Downstream Footprint
  - Standards -> Why Standards Matter
  - Standards -> Crude Oil Tests
  - Standards -> Gasoline Specifications
  - Standards -> Diesel Specifications
  - Standards -> Jet Fuel Specifications
  - Standards -> Residual and Bunker Fuel
  - Finished Products -> Diesel: On-Road, Off-Road, Marine, and Home Heat
  - Finished Products -> Jet Fuel and the Arrival of SAF
  - Finished Products -> Residual Fuel and the Bunker Market After IMO 2020
  - Transporting Oil -> IMO 2020 and the Transformation of Bunker Fuel
  - Transporting Oil -> Incoterms and Delivery Pricing
- Numerical example: A cargo priced attractively at the production basin can still destroy value if the nearest refinery is in turnaround for 30 days, the alternative freight path adds $4 per barrel, and the delivered quality misses the buyer's sulfur window.
- Market question answered: When does a nominal barrel of supply become a usable barrel for a specific refinery, product market, place, and time?
